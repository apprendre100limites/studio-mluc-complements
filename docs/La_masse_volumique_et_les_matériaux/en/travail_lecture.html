Density and materials

Level 1 · Simplified reading

Why does an iron block flow while a block of wood floats? Because iron has a larger density than water.

The density, recorded rho, is the mass divided by volume. It is expressed in kilograms per cubic metre or grams per cubic centimeter. One gram per cubic centimeter is equivalent to one thousand kilograms per cubic metre.

Each material has its own density. For example, aluminum has a density of two decimal seven grams per cubic centimetre, seven decimal nine iron, nine copper, eleven decimal three lead and nineteen decimal three gold.

A material can be recognized by its density. If an object has a density of less than one gram per cubic centimeter, it will float in the water. Wood floats, iron flows.

Metals can form alloys. An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements. For example, steel is iron with some carbon. In steel, small carbon atoms are inserted between iron atoms.

Alloys have different properties from pure metals. For example, stainless steel does not rust. Many Caledonian nickel is used to make them.

Composite materials combine at least two non-miscible materials. For example, reinforced concrete combines concrete and steel bars. Concrete resists crushing, and steel resists stretching. Together, they are more resistant than everyone else.

The density is useful for verifying the purity of a metal without melting it. For example, if an object has a density of two decimal seven grams per cubic centimeter, it is aluminum.

Choosing a material is choosing its properties. For example, an aeroplane must be light, a durable deck and a stainless sink. With density, we can check that something is gold, as in Archimedes' history.

These concepts are used in industry, construction, jewellery and research.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

The density, recorded rho, is the mass divided by volume. It is expressed in kilograms per cubic metre or grams per cubic centimeter. One gram per cubic centimeter is equivalent to one thousand kilograms per cubic metre. Water has a density of one gram per cubic centimeter, while air has about two decimal two kilograms per cubic metre.

Each material has a characteristic density. For example, aluminum has a density of two decimal seven grams per cubic centimetre, seven decimal nine iron, nine copper, eleven decimal three lead and nineteen decimal three gold. This property recognizes a material. An object of five hundred and forty grams per two hundred cubic centimetres has a density of two decimal seven grams per cubic centimetres, indicating that it is made of aluminum.

The density also predicts buoyancy. A solid object floats in water if its density is less than one gram per cubic centimeter. For example, wood floats, whereas iron sinks.

Metals can form alloys, which are mixtures of metal with other elements. Steel is an iron alloy with some carbon. In steel, carbon atoms are inserted between iron atoms. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, some of which are replaced by zinc atoms. Each alloy has its own properties. Stainless steel, which contains iron, chrome and nickel, does not rust. Much of Caledonian nickel is used to make stainless steel.

Composite materials combine at least two non-miscible materials. For example, reinforced concrete combines concrete and steel bars. Concrete resists crushing, while steel resists stretching. Together, they are stronger than everyone alone. The properties of a composite depend on the assembly. Carbon fiber and resin give light and solid parts, used in bicycles, aircraft or surfboards.

Density is useful in many areas. It allows you to choose the appropriate material for a specific application: an aeroplane must be light, a deck must be resistant, and a sink must be stainless. With density, one can check that a ingot is gold without melting it, as in Archimedes' history. These concepts are also used in industry, construction, jewellery and research.
