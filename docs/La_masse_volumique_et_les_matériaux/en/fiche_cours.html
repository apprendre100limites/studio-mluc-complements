Density and materials

Hi! Why does a block of iron sink, while a block of wood floats? Why is a gold ring so heavy for its size? More than two thousand years ago, a king wondered if his crown was pure gold. How do you know without melting it? The answer lies in two words: density.

Metals

The vast majority of known chemical elements are metals: iron, copper, aluminum, nickel, gold, and many others.

In nature, there are very few pure metals. Most are enclosed in ores, rocks from which the industry extracts the metal.

New Caledonia has one of the largest nickel reserves in the world. The ore is extracted in the mines and then processed in the factory to obtain the metal.

Takeaway. The vast majority of known chemical elements are metals.

Density

The density, denoted rhô, is the mass divided by the volume: rhô equals m divided by V.

It is expressed in kilograms per cubic meter, or in grams per cubic centimeter.

One gram per cubic centimetre is one thousand kilograms per cubic metre.

Water is one gram per cubic centimeter, and air about one decimal two kilograms per cubic meter.

Example: an aluminum cube with a side of two centimeters has a volume of eight cubic centimeters, and a mass of twenty-one decimal six grams. Its density: twenty-one decimal six divided by eight, or two decimal seven grams per cubic centimeter.

Each material has its own density: this is a characteristic property.

Aluminum, two point seven. Iron, seven point nine. Copper, nine. Lead, eleven point three. Now, nineteen decimal three.

Takeaway. Density is mass divided by volume.
Caution. One gram per cubic centimetre is equivalent to one thousand kilograms per cubic metre.

Metals & Alloys

An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements.

It is often stronger than pure metal. Steel, for example, is iron with a little carbon.

When the atoms added are smaller, they slip between the atoms of the metal: this is the insertion. In steel, small carbon atoms are inserted between the iron atoms.

When the atoms added are close in size, they take the place of certain atoms of the metal: this is substitution. Brass is copper, some atoms of which are replaced by zinc.

Every alloy has its properties. Stainless steel, made of iron, chromium and nickel, does not rust.

Much of New Caledonia's nickel is used to make it.

Takeaway. An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements.

Composite materials

A composite material combines at least two immiscible materials.

Reinforced concrete, for example, combines concrete and steel bars.

In reinforced concrete, concrete resists crushing, and steel resists stretching. Together, they do better than each one alone.

The properties of a composite depend on the assembly. Carbon fiber and resin give light and strong parts, used in bicycles, airplanes or surfboards.

Takeaway. A composite material combines at least two immiscible materials.

What's the point?

What's the point of all this? Not just to pass a check. Choosing a material means choosing its properties: lightweight for an aircraft, resistant for a bridge, stainless for a sink. And with the density, we can verify that an ingot is indeed gold, without melting it, as in the story of Archimedes. These concepts are used in industry, construction, jewellery and research.

Conclusion

To summarize: the density is worth the mass divided by the volume, and characterizes a material. An alloy is formed by insertion or substitution. A composite assembles immiscible materials.
