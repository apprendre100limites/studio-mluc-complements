Pharos Case 4/4: The Crime Scene

On August 8, 1303, in Alexandria, a catastrophe unfolded that destroyed the lighthouse. Testimonies diverge on the exact causes of the collapse.

Diverging testimonies

An earthquake and a tsunami were said to be the causes, along with a very tall tower built from enormous blocks.

Historians Maqrizi and Ibn Taghribardi report different versions of the event, while Ibn Battuta describes the lighthouse in a state where entry is no longer possible.

Key points. The testimonies are not all identical and diverge on the cause of the earthquake.
Caution. Do not blindly accept a single version of events without verifying sources.

The digital twin

Researchers build a digital twin of the lighthouse from scanned blocks.

They simulate loads, stresses, and falls to understand how and why the lighthouse collapsed.

Key points. The digital twin allows testing hypotheses without damaging the structure.
Caution. A digital test does not replace the real study of the monument.

Your mission and verdict

Each group builds two towers: one straight and one stepped, with a wide base and a lightweight top.

Loads are placed on the top, the table is shaken, and the platform is tilted to observe stability.

Key points. The aim is to compare the forms of towers and determine which one resists best
Be careful. Repeat each test in the same way for a fair comparison

What's the point?

This method allows us to understand how structures behave under stress. It is essential in engineering to test bridges, buildings or dams before construction. The trades concerned are the structural computing engineer, civil engineering engineer, architect and digital simulation specialist.

Conclusion

Using models and tests, we can understand past structures and improve modern constructions.
