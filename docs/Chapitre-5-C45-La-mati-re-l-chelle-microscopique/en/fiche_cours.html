The microscopic scale of matter

A glass of water seems continuous, but it is composed of very small particles. A drop contains more than a thousand billion billion molecules, a number difficult to imagine.

The particulate model

To better understand reality, scientists simplify it by modeling it; the model used for the material is called the particulate model.

In 1827, botanist Robert Brown observed tiny grains constantly moving in the water, while nobody touched them, which was explained by the Brownian movement of invisible water particles that shoved them.

To be remembered. The particulate model describes matter as a set of small moving particles.
Be careful. Do not believe that the grains observed by Brown are water particles; It's invisible particles that push them.

The state of matter

In the solid state, the particles are tight and tidy, each vibrates while remaining in its place, bound to its neighbours, giving the solid a clean shape.

In the liquid state, the particles remain tight but slide on each other, so that the liquid takes the form of its container.

In the gaseous state, the particles are very far away, move quickly in all directions and there is a vacuum between them, the gas then occupies all the available space.

Takeaway. In a solid the particles are squeezed and stored, in a liquid they slide, and in a gas they are very far apart and move quickly.
Caution. Avoid thinking that a gas has no void between its particles.

Gas & Compression

Between the particles of a gas there is a lot of vacuum, which makes it possible to bring them closer together; the gas is therefore compressible, as shown by depressing the plunger of a syringe without changing the number of particles.

The pressure of a gas comes from the shocks of its particles against the walls; the more shocks, the greater the pressure, and a higher temperature accelerates the particles, thus increasing the pressure.

Takeaway. A gas is compressible because its particles are separated by a lot of vacuum, and the pressure comes from the shocks of the particles against the walls.
Caution. Do not confuse gas compression with an increase in the number of particles.

Mixtures and state changes

When sugar dissolves in water, its particles slip between those of the water, forming a solution that is a mixture of solvent and solute particles.

Dissolve, melt or boil are physical transformations: particles remain the same, only their organization changes, a water molecule remaining the same in ice as in steam.

To be remembered. Dissolve a solute in a solvent creates a mixture where particles interpenetrate, and changes in state do not alter the particles themselves.
Be careful. Do not believe that molecules disappear when you melt or tip water.

What's the point?

The particulate model helps engineers design new materials by adjusting particle organization. A mechanic uses this principle when inflating a tire by compressing a gas. It also helps predict the behaviour of a material in industry, health and research.

Conclusion

The substance consists of moving particles which remain the same regardless of state, only their provisions change.
