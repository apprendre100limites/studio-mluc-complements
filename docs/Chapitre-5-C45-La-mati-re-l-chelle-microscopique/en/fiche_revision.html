The microscopic scale of matter

  • Matter consists of very small particles, invisible to the eye.
  • The particulate model describes the organization of particles according to the state of matter.
  • In a solid, particles are tightened, rowed and vibrate on site, giving a clean shape.
  • In a liquid, the particles remain tight but slide over each other, adopting the shape of the container.
  • In a gas, the particles are very distant, move quickly in all directions and the gas is compressible.
  • The pressure of a gas comes from the impacts of particles against walls and increases with temperature.
  • State changes change the organization of particles but not their nature.

What's the point?

The particulate model predicts the behaviour of a material without testing everything, helping to create new materials or optimize processes. Materials engineers, mechanics and health researchers use this model in industry, research and medicine.
