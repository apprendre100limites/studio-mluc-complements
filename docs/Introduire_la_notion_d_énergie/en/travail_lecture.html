Level 1 · Simplified reading

Energy never disappears. It transforms and transfers. A swing that stops heating the air. She does it because of friction.

Your body uses food energy. This energy is used to move and reflect. If you eat too little, you're tired. If you eat too much, the body stores the energy.

Machines don't create energy. They convert it from a source. A solar panel uses light. An engine uses fuel. The sun and wind are renewable. Oil and coal are non-renewable. In New Caledonia, electricity comes from fossil fuels. But the solar is progressing.

On a bike, energy becomes movement. The dynamo turns it into electricity. The lamp uses this electricity. We use an energy chain. These concepts are used in sport and health. Energy is never destroyed.

Level 2 · Standard reading

Energy is a pervasive theme, whether for solar energy, food or energy saving. This video explains what energy means, where our body comes from, and how energy conversions are described.

A pendulum has energy because it is moving or high. At the top, its speed is zero and its maximum altitude; at the lowest, its speed is maximum and its altitude is minimum. Gradually, it stops, but its energy has not disappeared.

Our body uses energy. The intake comes from food, while the needs correspond to the energy spent on moving. The needs are eight thousand three hundred and sixty-eight to ten thousand four hundred and sixty kilojoules a day. A suitable diet brings as much energy as the body spends.

Machines convert energy. Sources are quickly renewed, such as the sun, wind or water; Others, such as oil, coal or gas, exist in limited stock. In New Caledonia, electricity still comes from fossil fuels, but solar and wind are growing.

An energy chain describes conversions, for example on a bike: the chemical energy of food becomes muscle movement, the dynamo produces electricity, the lamp gives light. Energy is transferred and converted, without ever being destroyed.

These concepts apply in energy, health, building and sport, helping engineers, dieticians and electricians to design effective solutions. Next time you turn on a lamp, think about the energy chain that makes it shine.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

In the video, we start by wondering what energy really is. The example of the swing that stops shows that energy is not lost, it is simply transferred to the air by friction, which warms it a little bit. This idea of transfer explains why nothing disappears, but simply changes form.

Then we see that our body works like a small machine. Food brings us chemical energy, which the body uses to move, reflect and maintain its temperature. The needs of an adult are between eight thousand three hundred and sixty-eight and ten thousand four hundred and sixty kilojoules each day. If the intake is too low, you feel tired; If it is too important, the surplus is stored in the body. Thus, the balance between intake and expenditure is essential to stay fit.

The third part shows that machines do not create energy, they convert it. An engine transforms the energy of fuel, a solar panel that of light. Some sources, such as the sun, wind or water, are renewed as quickly as they are used, making them sustainable. Others, such as oil, coal or gas, are limited and eventually run out. In New Caledonia, the majority of electricity still comes from fossil fuels, but solar and wind energy are gaining ground.

Finally, the video describes energy chains using a bike equipped with a dynamo. The chemical energy of food becomes energy of movement, then electric energy thanks to dynamo, and finally light in the lamp, with a small loss of heat. This diagram shows how each conversion retains the amount of energy, even if its shape changes.

Understanding these principles serves many trades: an engineer designs solar farms, a dietitian adjusts food intakes, an electrician optimizes facilities. For example, knowing where energy comes from, how it is transformed and why some renewable sources help make more responsible choices in everyday life.

Key vocabulary

  • energy : Ability to do work, expressed in joules.
  • pendulum : Oscillating device used to measure time.
  • renewable : Ability to reproduce quickly or regenerate.
  • energy chain : Diagram illustrating energy conversions in a system.
  • contribution : Amount of energy supplied to the body.
