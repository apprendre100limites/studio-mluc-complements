Light

  • A primary source (sun, lamp, flame, screen) produces light, the Moon only diffuses it.
  • To see an object, it must be illuminated and the diffused light enters the eye.
  • In the air, the light moves straight in the form of rays.
  • Light travels very quickly: about 300,000 km/s, 8 minutes from the Sun, ~1/3 s from the Moon.
  • An opaque object blocks the light and creates a shadow whose size depends on the distance from the source.
  • Too intense light (sun, powerful laser, welding arc) can permanently damage the eye.
  • Specific protections (ecliptical bezels, welding mask) are required for these dangerous sources.

Figures and formulae

What's the point?

These concepts allow you to predict the shadows of a building, design glasses or study the light of the stars. Crafts such as architect, optician, astronomer or welder need them.

If only one sentence is retained

One sees an object if light coming from him enters the eye.
