Sound

  • Sound is a vibration, a rapid back‑and‑forth motion of matter particles.
  • A sound source is any object that vibrates, such as a guitar string or vocal cords.
  • Sound propagates from particle to particle in a material medium (air, water, solid) and does not travel in a vacuum.
  • In air at 20 °C, sound travels at about 340 m/s, faster in water and even faster in steel.
  • Sound level is measured in decibels (dB): whisper ≈30 dB, conversation 60 dB, heavy traffic 80 dB, concert 100‑110 dB.
  • From 85 dB onward, prolonged exposure can permanently damage hearing.
  • To protect ears, keep distance from speakers, take breaks, limit volume, and wear earplugs at concerts.

What is it used for?

These concepts help protect hearing and use sound in various fields such as health, music, film, or safety. They are employed by audiologists, sound engineers, ENT doctors, and sonographers.
