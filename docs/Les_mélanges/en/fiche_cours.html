Mixtures

Some mixtures are done without problems, others refuse to mix. This video explains the dissolution, the notion of limit and the difference between miscible and non-miscible liquids.

Dissolution

When you leave sugar in a glass of water and stir, sugar gradually disappears.

Sugar is the solute, water the solvent, and the mixture obtained is called a solution, which is homogeneous with the naked eye.

To be remembered. The solute dissolves in the solvent to form a homogeneous solution.
Be careful. Do not confuse solute (which dissolves) with solvent (which dissolves).

A limit?

Adding sugar to tea reaches a time when sugar no longer dissolves and stays at the bottom of the glass.

This maximum amount of solute that a litre of solvent may contain is called solubility, expressed in grams per litre, and when reached the solution is saturated.

To be remembered. Solubility is the limit of what can be dissolved in a litre of solvent, beyond which the solution is saturated.
Be careful. Not to believe that it can always dissolve more solute continuously, saturation prevents this.

Two liquids

When water and mint syrup are mixed, a single homogeneous liquid is obtained: both liquids are miscible.

On the other hand, water and oil form two distinct layers, a heterogeneous mixture, because they are unmiscible and oil floats over water.

To be remembered. If the mixture appears as a single liquid, the liquids are miscible; If it forms two layers, they are not miscible.
Be careful. Do not think that miscibility depends on the colour of the liquid, but on their ability to mix completely.

Recognize a mixture

The same substance can be very soluble in one solvent and very little in another, like sugar that dissolves well in water but almost not in oil.

To identify the type of mixture, just look at the number of visible phases: a single phase indicates a homogeneous mixture, several phases indicate a heterogeneous mixture.

To be remembered. The number of phases observed makes it possible to distinguish a homogeneous mixture from a heterogeneous mixture.
Be careful. Do not forget to identify the solute and solvent when a component is dissolved.

What's the point?

These concepts are useful in preparing specific solutions, as does the pharmacist who has to dose exactly the amount of solute.

A painter chooses the appropriate solvent according to the type of paint, and on an aquaculture farm the amount of salt dissolved in the water of the basins is controlled.

They are therefore employed in health, construction, agriculture and research.

To be remembered. Understanding dissolution, solubility and miscibility is indispensable in many occupations.

What's the point?

This knowledge allows to prepare solutions with the right amount of solute, to choose the right solvent for a paint or to control the concentration of salt in the water of an aquaculture farm. They are necessary in health, construction, agriculture and research. Pharmacists, painters and farmers use these concepts daily.

Conclusion

Solute dissolves in the solvent to give a solution, solubility sets the dissolution limit, and two liquids may or may not be miscible.
