Changes in the state of matter

Level 1 · Simplified reading

State changes are when matter changes shape. For example, when an ice cube melts, it's fusion. Water that becomes steam is vaporization. Liquid steam is liquefaction. And liquid to solid is solidification.

When pure water is heated, its temperature rises to 100 degrees Celsius. It's boiling. Meanwhile, steam bubbles appear throughout the liquid. Temperature remains stable during this state change.

To cool water, its temperature drops to zero degrees Celsius. It's solidification. The liquid becomes solid.

If the water is salty, the temperature continues to rise during boiling. It's because there's a mixture.

The atmospheric pressure influences the boiling temperature of the water. The higher the pressure, the lower the pressure, the sooner the water ends.

State changes are important. They allow cooling with refrigerators, generating electricity in thermal power plants, and air conditioning of buildings.

Level 2 · Standard reading

The changes in water condition are fascinating. When water heats, it rises to a temperature of up to 100 degrees Celsius, where it begins to vaporize. During this phase called boiling, the temperature remains constant despite the increase in heat. This period without temperature change is called a temperature range.

On the other hand, if pure water is cooled, its temperature drops to zero degrees Celsius, where it solidifies. Again, the temperature range appears.

For mixtures, such as salt water, the situation is different. The boiling occurs at a higher temperature and the temperature continues to increase during the vaporization phase.

Air pressure also influences the boiling of water. The higher the altitude, the lower the pressure, the lower the water. On the other hand, in a coconut-minute, the pressure increases and the water ends at a temperature greater than 100 degrees Celsius.

Understanding these state changes is useful in everyday life. For example, when cooking, the bubbles appear during boiling. These bubbles are actually water vapour droplets that form in hot water.

In summary, pure water transforms from solid to liquid at zero degrees Celsius and from steam liquid at 100 degrees Celsius. These temperature changes are important to understand how many devices and processes work in our daily lives.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

State changes are transformations that matter can undergo. For example, when an ice cube melts in a glass, it is a fusion, which occurs at zero degrees Celsius. On the other hand, when water turns into steam, it's vaporization. If the steam meets a cold surface, it liquefies. Finally, when water turns into ice, it's solidification.

When pure water is heated, its temperature increases until it reaches 100 degrees Celsius, where it comes to a boil. During this phase, the temperature does not change despite continuous heating, forming a bearing. This is because energy is used to turn water into steam rather than to increase its temperature.

The distinction between boiling and evaporation is important. Evaporation occurs on the surface of the water at any temperature, while boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a specified temperature.

When a pure liquid is cooled, its temperature decreases to zero degrees Celsius, where it enters solidification. Again, during this bearing, the temperature does not change as the ice forms.

For mixtures, such as salt water, there is no temperature level during boiling. The temperature continues to rise.

Air pressure also affects boiling temperature. The higher the pressure, the lower the temperature of the water. For example, at the top of Mont Blanc, the water ends at about eighty-four degrees. On the other hand, in a cocotte-minute, the pressure is higher and the water ends at more than a hundred degrees, allowing to cook food faster.

Everything we have just learned has practical applications. For example, the cold of our fridge or air conditioning comes from changes in condition. A liquid that evaporates cools, and a compressed gas liquefies. In a thermal power plant, water becomes steam and runs a turbine that produces electricity.

Key vocabulary

  • merger : the change of state of a solid into liquid
  • spraying : the change of state of a liquid into gas
  • liquefaction : the change of state of a liquid gas
  • solidification : the change of state of a liquid in solid
  • temperature level : constant temperature during a change of state
