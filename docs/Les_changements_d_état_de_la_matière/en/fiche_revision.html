Changes in the state of matter

  • The transition from solid to liquid is called melting, and from liquid to gas, vaporization.
  • The passage from gas to liquid is called liquefaction, and from liquid to solid, solidification.
  • When a pure body changes state, its temperature remains stable: it is a temperature plateau.
  • Evaporation is slow and occurs at the surface, while boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a precise temperature.
  • A mixture, such as salt water, does not have a temperature plateau during boiling.
  • The boiling temperature of the water decreases when the pressure decreases, for example at altitude.
  • In a pressure cooker, the pressure is higher and the water boils above one hundred degrees.
  • A pure body has a temperature plateau, a mixture does not.

What's the point?

It is used to understand how cold the fridge or air conditioning is, and how a thermal power plant produces electricity with steam. It is needed in the trades of refrigerator, plant technician and meteorologist.

If we only remember one sentence

A pure body keeps a constant temperature as it changes state, whereas a mixture does not.
