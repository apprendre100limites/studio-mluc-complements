Introduction

Alexandria, in Egypt, more than two thousand years ago. At night, a ship approaches the coast in front of reefs and rocks, a dangerous coast. In the distance, a light: an immense tower, the tallest that these sailors have ever seen. This tower is the Lighthouse of Alexandria, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Today, it has disappeared.

Key points. The Lighthouse of Alexandria is one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

Welcome, investigators

You join the team at the Alexandria Studies Center, which researchers call the Céalex. It is a research unit of the National Center for Scientific Research, located in Alexandria. Since 1994, these scientists have been investigating the lighthouse. In this four-episode series, you will redo part of their investigation and verify some of their findings yourself.

Key points. The Céalex has been investigating the lighthouse since 1994.

The construction and its cost

The construction starts around 297 BC under King Ptolemy I. It will not see the end of the works. The lighthouse is completed under his successor, Ptolemy II, between 283 and 281 BC. It took only a decade to raise this tower, a record time for the era. It costs a lot: the Roman writer Pliny the Elder speaks of eight hundred talents. A talent is a unit of mass, and here it weighs a little more than twenty-one kilograms. Eight hundred talents therefore represent about seventeen tonnes of silver. A century and a half earlier, the Parthenon in Athens had cost a little more than twelve tonnes of silver. The lighthouse is more expensive.

Takeaway: The lighthouse cost about seventeen tonnes of silver.
Be careful. Do not forget that the project was not completed by Ptolemy I.

A unique lighthouse in the Mediterranean

The lighthouse is located on the island of Pharos, right in front of the port of Alexandria. It is from this name that our word "lighthouse" comes. He signals the city from far away and controls the entrance to the Grand Port. According to Pharos researchers, it measured about one hundred and three meters, as tall as a building with about thirty floors. Its look is unique in the Mediterranean: three superimposed sections, and a statue at the top. This silhouette is so famous that other cities copy the idea. At Ostia, the port of Rome, Emperor Claudius built a very high tower, according to the writer Suetonius, "on the model of the lighthouse of Alexandria".

To remember. The lighthouse measured about one hundred and three meters and had a unique look in the Mediterranean.

The fall

For one thousand six hundred years, he remained standing. Then comes the eighth of August, one thousand three hundred and three. A note written in the annals of a city in southern France recounts that a great earthquake struck Alexandria and brought down the lighthouse, as well as about a third of the city. Then the lighthouse is abandoned. Its stones serve as a quarry for a time. Then what's left of it disappears under the waters of the Mediterranean.

To remember. The lighthouse fell on August 8, 1303.
Be careful. Do not say that the earthquake struck a town in southern France; it struck Alexandria.

Four families of indices

This is the problem: we no longer have the tower, and we lack precise documents to know how it was made. So the researchers conduct the investigation with four families of clues. First family: texts. Authors of Antiquity and the Middle Ages have described the lighthouse, sometimes very precisely. Second family: coins. For almost a century, Alexandria engraved the lighthouse on its coins. Third family: the remains. At the bottom of the water, at the foot of a medieval fort, thousands of blocks of stone are still waiting. Fourth family: the digital model. With the support of the Dassault Systèmes Foundation, the team is building a digital twin of the lighthouse, i.e. a three-dimensional copy that can be observed and tested on a computer.

To remember. The four families of clues are texts, coins, remains, and the digital model.
Be careful. Do not confuse the remnants with the digital model.

Three questions

And three questions will guide our entire investigation. What did the lighthouse really look like? How was it built? And why did he fall?

Takeaway. The three questions relate to the appearance, construction and fall of the lighthouse.

What is it used for?

What's a lighthouse for? To answer this question, technicians write a specification: a list of what an object must do. It contains a main function, the raison d'être of the object, and functions of constraint, which it must respect in order to perform properly. Main function: to guide ships. In other words, guide ships by night along a dangerous coast. And here are the functions of the headlight constraint: constraint one: to be seen from a very far distance; constraint two: to be visible at night; stress three: resisting the sea and the wind; stress four: stand for a very long time. Next, the survey itself shows occupations. Isabelle Hairy, who runs the Pharos project, is an architect and archaeologist at the CNRS. He worked alongside historians, divers and digital specialists. Conclusion

The Lighthouse of Alexandria was a tower that guided ships and vanished after its collapse.
