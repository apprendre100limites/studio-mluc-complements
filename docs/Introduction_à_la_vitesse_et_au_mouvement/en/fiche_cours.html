Introduction to speed and motion

In this sheet, you will learn how to describe a motion, calculate a speed, and understand why turns are dangerous. The concepts covered will help you perceive the world around you better.

The trajectory

When an object moves, the set of positions it occupies successively is called its trajectory.

If the successive positions are aligned, the motion is linear.

If they form a circle or an arc of a circle, the motion is circular.

To remember. The trajectory is the set of successive positions occupied by a moving object.

Calculating a speed

Average speed is the distance traveled divided by the time.

For example, one hundred fifty kilometres traveled in two hours gives seventy‑five kilometres per hour.

Instantaneous speed is the one shown by the speedometer at a specific instant.

The International System unit for speed is the metre per second, but kilometres per hour are also used.

To convert kilometres per hour to metres per second, you divide by three point six; in the opposite direction, you multiply.

To remember. Average speed is distance divided by time, and you divide km/h by 3.6 to obtain m/s.
Caution. Confusing average speed with instantaneous speed.

Magnitude, direction and sense

Instant speed has three characteristics: a value, a direction and a direction.

Your meter only gives the value.

A movement is uniform when the speed value remains constant, even if the direction changes.

In a rectilinear motion, the direction does not change, but the value may change.

Takeaway. The speed has a value, a direction and a direction, and the movement can be uniform or vary in value while remaining rectilinear.
Caution. Believe that the direction changes during a uniform movement.

Turning

In a turn, it is the direction of the speed that changes.

At high speed, the vehicle tends to continue straight ahead.

The tighter a turn, the slower you have to go.

It is better to slow down before entering the turn than to brake in the middle, because then you risk getting off the road.

Takeaway. In a bend, you slow down before entering to avoid getting off the road.
Caution. Brake in the middle of a corner instead of slowing down before.

What's the point?

These notions are used to pass a test and to better understand the world around you. They are useful in everyday life, especially to analyze our movements and respect speed limits. They are indispensable in the fields of road engineering, road safety, aviation and sport.

Conclusion

To summarize: the trajectory is the set of positions, the average speed is the distance divided by the duration, the speed has a value, a direction and a direction, and in a turn we slow down before entering.
