Level 1 · Simplified reading

When an object moves, its trajectory is called the set of positions it occupies. For example, car headlights aligned on a straight road form a straight path.

To calculate the average speed, the distance travelled is divided by the duration. For example, one hundred and fifty kilometers in two hours is equivalent to seventy-five kilometers per hour.

The instantaneous speed indicated by the meter is the speed at a specific time. The unit of speed is the meter per second, but we also use the kilometer per hour.

Instantaneous speed has three characteristics: a value, a direction, and a direction. Your meter only gives the value.

A motion is uniform if the speed value remains constant, even if the direction changes.

In a turn, the direction of the speed changes. We need to slow down before entering a bend to avoid getting off the road.

The average speed of a cyclist who travels thirty kilometers in an hour and a half is twenty kilometers per hour.

These concepts are used in transport, road safety, aviation and sport.

Level 2 · standard reading

A car, a cyclist, a runner: everything that moves has a speed. But just how fast are we talking? Your meter reads ninety. Did you drive at ninety the whole way? Obviously not: there were turns, lights, hills.

In this video, you will learn how to describe a movement, calculate a speed, and understand why turns are dangerous. When an object moves, the set of positions it successively occupies is called its trajectory. If the successive positions are aligned, the movement is rectilinear: for example, car headlights, at night, on a straight road. If they form a circle or an arc of a circle, the movement is circular, like a ferris wheel nacelle.

Average speed is the distance travelled divided by duration. For example, one hundred and fifty kilometres travelled in two hours: one hundred and fifty divided by two, seventy-five kilometres per hour. Another example: in two thousand nine, Usain Bolt ran a hundred meters in nine decimal fifty-eight seconds. Its average speed is about ten decimal four metres per second, almost 38 kilometres per hour. Be careful not to confuse. The average speed is for the entire route. Instant speed is what your meter indicates at a specific moment. The international unit of speed is the meter per second, but we also use the kilometer per hour. To pass miles per hour at meters per second, you divide by three decimal six. In the other direction, you multiply.

Instant speed has three characteristics: a value, direction, and direction. Your meter only gives the value. A movement is uniform when the value of the speed remains constant, even if the direction changes, as a regular speed ride. In a straight motion, the direction does not change, but the value can change, like a car that accelerates in a straight line.

In a turn, the direction of speed changes. The wheels change it. But at high speed, the vehicle tends to continue straight ahead. The tighter a turn, the slower you go. It is better to slow down before entering the turn than brake in the middle, because then you risk the exit road.

What's all this for? Not just to pass control. A road engineer designs turns and sets speed limits based on what you just learned. Radar measures vehicle speed to make roads safer. And a sports coach analyses the speed of his athletes to advance them. These concepts are used in transport, road safety, aviation and sport.

To sum up: the trajectory is the set of positions. Average speed is the distance divided by duration. Speed has value, direction and meaning. And in a turn, we slow down before entering.

Key vocabulary

  • Trajectory : All the successive positions that an object occupies during its movement.
  • Speed : The speed with which an object moves.
  • Instant speed : The speed a meter indicates at a precise moment of the journey.
  • Transfer : A change of direction of movement.
  • Distance : The length travelled by an object.
