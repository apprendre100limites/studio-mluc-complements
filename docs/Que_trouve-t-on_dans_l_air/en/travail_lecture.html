What do we find in the air?

Consign. Choose the level of reading that suits you. Read the text, then note the essentials in the right column.

Level 1 · Simplified reading

Air is a mixture of gas. It contains mainly nitrogen (sixty-eight percent) and dioxygen (twenty-one percent).

Dioxygen is important for combustion and rust. It helps candles burn and causes rust on metals.

To separate the gases from the air, it is cooled until it becomes a liquid. Then it is warmed gently to remove each gas at its freezing point temperature.

The air has a mass. A swollen balloon is heavier than a deflated balloon. A liter of air has about a gram two of mass.

The mass of the air may vary. Wet air is lighter than dry air. The temperature also influences the mass of the air, a litre of hot air is lighter than a litre of cold air.

Separating gases from air is useful for industry. Dioxygen is used in hospitals and dinitrogen to store food. Air mass is important for aviation.

Level 2 · Standard reading

Air is a mixture of gas. It contains approximately seventy-eight percent of nitrogen, twenty-one percent of dioxygen and one percent of other gases such as argon. Dioxygen is important because it feeds combustion and causes rust. Dinitrogen does not react with other substances.

To separate the gases from the air, the liquefy is started by cooling it to about one hundred and ninety degrees. Then we heat this liquid gently. Each gas returns to the gaseous state at its own temperature: dinitrogen starts first, followed by dioxygen. Industry uses this process to store dioxygen, for example for hospitals.

The air has a mass. A swollen balloon is heavier than a deflated balloon. A litre of air has a mass of about one gram two. In a three-by-four-metre room, two-by-fifty metres high, there are about thirty-six kilograms of air.

The mass of the air varies according to the conditions. Wet air is a little lighter than dry air, and a litre of hot air contains less molecules than a litre of cold air. That's why the warm air rises.

Separating gases from air is an industry that serves industry, hospitals and aviation. In conclusion, air is mainly composed of nitrogen and dioxygen.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

The air around us is composed of a mixture of gas. It consists mainly of seventy-eight percent dinitrogen and twenty-one percent dioxygen. The rest, one percent, is composed of other gases such as argon.

To understand how these gases can be separated, air can be liquefied by cooling to about one hundred and ninety degrees Celsius. Then, by gradually heating this liquid, each gas returns to the gaseous state at its own temperature. Dinitrogen starts first, followed by dioxygen. This technique is used in industry to store dioxygen, for example for hospitals.

The air has a mass, even if we don't see it. A swollen balloon weighs more than a deflated balloon. To prove this, one can weigh a deflated balloon, inflate it and weigh it again. The difference is due to the mass of air added. A litre of air has a mass of about one gram two. In a three-by-four-metre room, two-by-fifty metres high, there are about thirty-six kilograms of air.

The air mass may vary depending on the conditions. Wet air is a little lighter than dry air, and hot air is less dense than cold air. That's why the hot air rises, like in a hot air balloon.

This knowledge is useful in many areas, such as industry, weather and aviation. They help us understand how the air around us works.

Key vocabulary

  • Dinitrogen : A low reactive air gas representing about 78% of its composition.
  • dioxygen : A gas that makes up the air, essential for breathing and combustion, represents about 21% of its composition.
  • mass : The quantity of material contained in a volume of air, measured in grams per litre.
  • composition : The proportion of the different gases that make up the air, mainly dinitrogen and dioxygen.
