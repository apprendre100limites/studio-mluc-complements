What do we find in the air?

Hey! You don't see it, you don't feel it, but you're immersed in it: the air. What's in it? Sometimes people say that air is empty. It's not true: air is a mixture of gas, and it even has a mass.

Air composition

Air is a mixture. It contains approximately seventy-eight percent of nitrogen, twenty-one percent of dioxygen, and one percent of other gases, such as argon.

Take a hundred molecules of air: about seventy-eight are dinitrogen, and twenty-one dioxygen. So there is almost four times as much nitrogen as dioxygen.

To be remembered. There is about four times more nitrogen than dioxygen in the air.

Separate Gases

In detail, air also contains argon, a little bit of carbon dioxide, and water vapour, in varying amounts.

To separate these gases, we start by liquefying the air by cooling it very hard, at about one hundred and ninety degrees. It becomes a very cold liquid, which smokes in the open air.

Then we heat this liquid gently. Each gas returns to the gas state at its own temperature: the dinitrogen starts first, at less than one hundred and ninety-six degrees, then the dioxygen, at less than one hundred and eighty-three.

Liquefied, gas takes up much less space. This is why industry stores dioxygen, for example for hospitals.

To be remembered. Dinitrogen starts first and dioxygen last when gas is separated.

The air has a mass

A deflated balloon and a swollen balloon do not have the same mass. Put them on a scale: the inflated one is heavier.

To prove it, you weigh the deflated ball, swell it without adding anything else, then weigh it again. The mass has increased: it's the air you added.

Under normal conditions, one litre of air has a mass of approximately one decimal two to one decimal three grams.

In a room three by four meters high, two meters high, there is thirty thousand liters of air. That's about thirty-six kilograms, about the mass of a ten-year-old.

To be remembered. A litre of air has a mass of about one gram two.

What's the point?

Separation of gases from air is important to industry. Dioxygen is used in hospitals, and dinitrogen is used to store certain foods. The mass of the air influences the aircraft's lift.

Conclusion

To sum up: air is a mixture, especially dinitrogen and dioxygen. We separate his gases by liquefying him and then warming him up. And a liter of air weighs about a gram two.
