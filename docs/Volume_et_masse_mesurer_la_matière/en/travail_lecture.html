Volume and mass: measuring matter

Consign. Choose the level of reading that suits you. Read the text, then note the essentials in the right column.

Level 1 · Simplified reading

Volume is the place an object takes. The volume is measured in cubic metres, litres, or millilitres. A liter is a cubic decimeter. A millilitre is a cubic centimeter. For a liquid, a graduated test piece is used. Graduation is read at the bottom of the curve formed by the liquid, placing its eye at the level.

A rock has a volume that can be measured by first noting the volume of water, then diving the rock and noting the new volume. The difference is the volume of the rock.

There are three states of matter: solid, liquid, and gas. A solid keeps its shape. A liquid takes the form of its container. A gas occupies all the space.

To weigh a liquid, first place the empty container on a balance, then press "tar". Then the liquid is poured. The scale indicates its mass. A litre of water weighs a kilogram. A millilitre of water weighs one gram.

The mass of a liquid is proportional to its volume. For water, a hundred millilitres weigh one hundred grams. For oil, a hundred millilitres weigh about ninety-two grams.

Volume and mass are important in everyday life. A truck has a maximum load in kilograms and a limited volume in cubic metres. A Logistician must think of both.

Level 2 · Standard reading

Volume is the place a matter takes. This volume is measured in cubic metres, litres or millilitres. A liter is a cubic decimeter and a millilitre, a cubic centimeter. A graduated test piece is used to measure the volume of a liquid. Place the test piece on a table, place the eye at the level of the liquid and read the graduation at the bottom of the curve formed by the liquid. For a solid, like a rock, we note the volume of water alone, we dive the rock, then we note the new volume. The difference between the two measurements gives the volume of the rock.

There are three states of matter: solid, liquid and gas. A solid keeps its shape, a liquid takes the shape of its container, and a gas occupies all the space it is offered.

To weigh a liquid, first place the empty container on the balance, press the tare and pour the liquid. The balance then indicates the mass of the liquid. A litre of water has a mass of one kilogram, so a millilitre of water weighs one gram. However, one litre of oil weighs about nine hundred and twenty grams, which is less than water.

The mass is proportional to the volume of the same substance. With water, one hundred millilitres weigh one hundred grams and two hundred and fifty millilitres, two hundred and fifty grams. With oil, a hundred millilitres weigh about ninety-two grams and two hundred and fifty, about two hundred and thirty. This report has a name, density.

Volume and mass are important in many trades. A logistician must think of both: a truck full of feathers is full before being heavy, a stone truck is heavy before being full. A pharmacy preparer measures volumes of liquids, a cook weighs his ingredients, and a logistician loads his trucks.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

In this video, we learn how to measure the volume and mass of objects and liquids. Volume is the place a matter takes. The volume is measured in cubic metres, litres or millilitres. For a liquid, a graduated test piece is used. You put the test piece on a table, look at the edge and read the measurement. For a solid, the volume of water is noted before and after the solid is immersed.

There are three states of matter: solid, liquid and gas. A solid keeps its shape, a liquid takes that of the container, and a gas occupies all the space.

To weigh a liquid, first place the empty container on a balance, press "tare" to reset the display to zero, then pour the liquid. Water is used as a reference: a litre of water weighs one kilogram.

Mass and volume are proportional for the same substance. For example, one hundred millilitres of water weigh one hundred grams, and two hundred and fifty millilitres, two hundred and fifty grams. However, this does not apply to all liquids. A litre of oil weighs about nine hundred and twenty grams, which is less than a litre of water.

This knowledge is used in everyday life, as to fill a glass of water and know its mass. It can also be used in trades such as logistics, pharmacy or kitchen.

Key vocabulary

  • cubic metre Volume unit in the international system.
  • test piece : Laboratory instrument used to measure the volume of liquids.
  • density : Relationship between the mass and volume of a substance.
  • tare : Action to reset the balance before weighing an object.
  • proportional : Refers to a relationship where an increase in the quantity of one variable results in a proportional increase in the other.
