Dolines in Turkey: when limestone disappears under our feet

  • Collapse dolines are circular depressions created in the dissolved limestone.
  • They are formed in karst environments where CO2-laden water forms carbonic acid that attacks calcium carbonate.
  • Chemical erosion is very slow, but over thousands of years it creates cavities that can collapse when the roof is no longer sustained.
  • In Konya, the water table declined by about 24 m between 1893 and 2008 and is now falling by 4-5 m per year, accelerating collapses.
  • The experience of vinegar shows that calcium carbonate reacts with a strong acid, releasing CO2 and dissolving the rock.
  • Maps of risk areas help avoid building over fragile cavities and help farmers choose their land.
  • Knowledge of dolines also helps to manage groundwater, protect sources and prevent pollution.

What's the point?

This knowledge makes it possible to draw up maps of risk areas to avoid building roads, hangars or houses over fragile cavities and to guide farmers in choosing their plots. They are also essential for geologists, hydrogeology engineers and urban planners to manage groundwater, protect drinking water sources and prevent pollution in karst regions.
