Is the water we drink pure?

The water you drink isn't always pure. This course shows you how to prove the presence of water and identify mixtures.

Water in food

A tomato, milk, bread: almost everything you eat or drink contains water. In a tomato, there are more than nine tenths.

How can I prove it? With a white powder: anhydrous copper sulphate. In contact with water, it becomes blue.

The test is simple. You put down some white powder, you add the liquid to test, and you look. If it turns blue, there's water. This powder is toxic: you only handle it with your teacher.

To be remembered. Water is present in almost all foods, and anhydrous copper sulphate can be detected by becoming blue.
Be careful. Handle anhydrous copper sulphate alone, as it is toxic.

Dissolved salts

When you pour salt into the water and move, it disappears. They say it's dissolved. The water remains transparent, but the salt is still there.

How can we find what's dissolved? By getting the water out. If you heat mineral water, the water vaporizes from liquid to vapour. And there's still a white deposit at the bottom.

The principle is to heat up. The water vaporizes, and the salts remain on site. This repository was dissolved in water.

In seawater, there are about thirty-five grams of dissolved salt per litre. In salt marshes, water is allowed to evaporate to recover salt.

On a bottle of mineral water, the label lists dissolved minerals: calcium, magnesium, sodium. It also indicates the dry residue, which is what remains when all the water has evaporated.

To be remembered. Evaporation is used to trace the dissolved salts, which form a deposit at the bottom, and the label of the beverages lists these residues.
Be careful. To think that the salt disappears completely when it is dissolved, while it is simply dispersed in the water.

Dissolved gases

In sparkling water, there is a dissolved gas. You see it when you open the bottle: bubbles appear.

This gas is carbon dioxide, also called carbon dioxide. It is colourless and odourless: you cannot recognize it either in your eye or in your nose.

To detect it, lime water is used. She's clear at first. If you put carbon dioxide in it, it becomes cloudy: it becomes white and milky.

Dissolved oxygen is also present in rivers, lakes and oceans. It comes from the air, and it is he who breathes fish.

To be remembered. Carbon dioxide dissolved in beverages causes bubbles, and lime water reveals it in white colouring.
Be careful. Try to recognize carbon dioxide by visual observation because of its lack of odour and colour.

Pure water and mixture

For a chemist, a pure body contains only one substance. A mixture contains several. Tap water or mineral water contain water, salts and dissolved gases: they are mixtures.

The purest water is called distilled water. It is obtained by distillation and contains almost nothing dissolved. We use it in the lab or irons, not for drinking every day.

Last tool: pH, which measures acidity. Between zero and seven, one solution is acidic. On seven, she's neutral. Above seven, it is basic. Clean water is neutral.

To be remembered. Mineral water is a mixture, distilled water is closest to purity, and pH determines the acidity of a solution.
Be careful. Consolidate tap water (mix) with distilled water (almost pure) by operating only in the current direction.

What's the point?

This knowledge allows you to check the quality of the tap water before drinking it. It is essential in analytical laboratories to search for dissolved substances. Techs, labs and hydrologists use it on a daily basis to ensure public health, particularly in Nouméa, where IRD researchers are studying the quality of lagoon water.

Conclusion

The water you drink is a mixture, and only distilled water is almost pure.
