Level 1 · Simplified reading

When you press a switch, the lamp lights up. But nothing moves with the naked eye. In the wires, the current moves. For the lamp to shine, it is necessary to connect its two terminals to the battery with conductor wires. If the chain is cut, the current does not pass and the lamp remains turned off. As soon as the chain closes, it forms a loop and the current circulates. Energy goes from battery to lamp. You still need two sons, not one.

The two-point components are called dipoles. We represent them with standardized symbols. A good diagram uses straight lines and straight angles. It must show a closed loop. When the dipoles are in series, they're on the same loop. If one of them stops, everything goes off. By-pass, each dipole has its own branch. If one branch opens, the others continue to work. This is how the house sockets are connected.

Level 3 · in-depth reading

For a lamp to light up, it is necessary to connect its two terminals to those of the battery with conductive wires. If the chain is cut, by an open switch, nothing happens. As soon as the chain closes on itself and forms a loop, the current circulates and the lamp shines. Electrical energy is transferred from the battery to the lamp. Many think that only one thread would be enough. In reality, it takes two: the current makes a loop, it does not stop in the lamp.

Then, to draw a circuit, the two-point components call dipoles: battery, lamp, switch, motor. They are drawn with standardized symbols, the same worldwide. To make a good diagram, you must use these symbols, draw the wires in straight lines with straight angles, and check that you get a closed loop.

Then there's the show and the bypass. Serial dipoles follow each other on the same loop. If you open the switch, or if a grille lamp, everything goes off, because the buckle is off. With bypass dipoles, each lamp is on its own branch, and each branch forms a loop with the battery. Open the switch on one branch makes the lamp turn off, but the other one continues to shine. Their operations are independent. That's why the sockets of your house are bypassed: a lamp that grills does not turn off the fridge.

In a complex circuit, serial dipoles form branches, and these branches are themselves associated by bypass. On one branch, two lamps in series, and one lamp alone on the other. An electrician designs bypass installations, so that each outlet works alone. Understanding that a circuit is a loop is why you cut the current before you fix it. To sum up: a circuit is a closed loop of drivers. We draw it with standard symbols. In series, the dipoles are connected. By-pass, they're independent.

Key vocabulary

  • loop : In electricity, it is the closed path that follows the current.
  • Switch : Device that opens or closes the current loop.
  • dipole : Component with two terminals such as a battery, lamp or motor.
  • series : Arrangement where the dipoles are placed one after the other on the same loop.
  • diversion : Provision where each dipole has its own independent branch.
