At the end of October 2026, the Vera C. Rubin Observatory spotted object 2026 ED138, a potential new dwarf planet in the Solar System.

A distant object named 2026 ED138 was spotted at the edge of the Solar System and could become a new dwarf planet. This sheet explains what a dwarf planet is, how this object was discovered and what remains to be verified to confirm its status.

What is a dwarf planet?

A dwarf planet is a star that revolves around the Sun as a planet, but is smaller and shares its orbit with other objects.

To be qualified as a dwarf planet, a body must meet three conditions: to rotate around the Sun, to be massive enough for its gravity to make it spherical, and not to have cleaned its orbital neighborhood. The eight planets in the solar system pass the three tests, while the dwarf planets fail only the third. Pluto is the best known example since 2006.

To be remembered. A dwarf planet revolves around the Sun, is round due to its gravity, and has not cleared its orbit.
Be careful. Do not confuse the orbit clearance condition with the simple object size.

How was the object spotted?

The object 2026 ED138 was discovered in data from Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile, whose telescope photographes the sky every night.

The astronomer Matthew Holman identified it on 13 October 2026, after the observatory had already captured thirty images between March and July 2026 and earlier images date back to 2016. These multiple observations allowed us to track its displacement and calculate its orbit, located at approximately 46 astronomical units of the Sun, which is further than Pluto on average.

To be remembered. The object was spotted thanks to numerous images of the Rubin Observatory, which enabled the determination of its orbit.
Be careful. Do not think that the object was discovered in one observation.

What remains to be checked

Its size is estimated between 600 and 1500 km, but this fork remains too wide to know if it is large enough to be spherical.

Infrared observations, for example with the James Webb space telescope, are needed to accurately measure its size. Its orbit, inclined about 25° from the planet plane, could be influenced by Neptune. So far, 2026 ED138 remains a candidate in the category of dwarf planets.

To be remembered. The exact size of 2026 ED138 must be measured to confirm its dwarf status.
Be careful. Do not conclude that it is a dwarf planet before having the size measurements.

Conclusion

In short, a dwarf planet revolves around the Sun, is round and has not cleaned its orbit; 2026 ED138 is a candidate discovered through the Rubin Observatory, but its size still needs to be measured to confirm its status.
